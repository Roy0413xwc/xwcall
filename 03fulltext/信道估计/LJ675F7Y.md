Abstract—This paper proposes a fast and robust graph-based
wavenumber-domain approach for channel estimation in holo-
graphic MIMO (HMIMO) systems. Unlike conventional angular-
domain methods—prone to mutual coupling , power leakage , and
sampling redundancy —our framework resolves HMIMO’s high-
dimensional challenges by introducing a wavenumber-domain
basis via orthogonal Fourier harmonics (FHs), eliminating depen-
dencies on antenna density. By reformulating channel estimation
as its sparse recovery counterpart, we model clustered sparsity
using an elliptic Markov random ﬁeld (EMRF), upon which
a graph-cut swap expansion (GCSE) algorithm is developed,
leveraging graph-theoretic optimizations for fast convergence
and low complexity. Simulations demonstrate that our method
achieves robust performance against mutual coupling, varying
SNRs, and antenna density with drastically less computing time.
Index Terms —Holographic MIMO, channel estimation,
wavenumber domain, compressed sensing, mutual coupling.

To answer this intriguing question, we refer to the Fourier
plane-wave series expansion in [33], which demonstrates that
the degrees of freedom (DoFs) of HMIMO are solely deter-
mined by the array sizes and carrier frequency [33, Eq. (48)].
When the antenna spacing decreases below half a wavelength
for ﬁxed array sizes, increasing the number of antennas does
not enhance angular resolution. Instead, a large number of
redundant zero entries are introduced into the angular domain,
which only expands the legitimate boundaries of the angular-
domain region without contributing to the array’s spatial
resolution. Therefore, the potential structured sparsity of the
HMIMO channel is difﬁcult to make out in the angular
domain. Additionally, strong mutual coupling will be non-
negligible due to the extremely dense antenna arrangement
in the HMIMO array, which acts as distortion and expansion
effects on the sparse-domain channel.
In this case, we shift our focus to the wavenumber-domain
representation as speciﬁed in [7] and [33] to explore the
potential clustered sparsity inherent in HMIMO channels. To
the best of our knowledge, limited efforts have been made to
examine the sparsity of HMIMO channels in the wavenumber
domain, particularly for their efﬁcient channel estimation
algorithms in the presence of the mutual coupling effect.
In a nutshell, the motivation of this work is ignited by the
aforementioned question, “ Can there also be angular-domain
sparsity in HMIMO channels? ” We aim to adequately address
this intriguing question by exploring the structured clustered
sparsity in HMIMO channels from a novel perspective –
the wavenumber domain – for achieving efﬁcient and robust
channel estimation for HMIMO systems. The contributions of
this paper can be summarized as follows.
1) We investigate the uplink channel estimation in the
HMIMO system. To examine the presence of sparsity in
HMIMO channels characterized by Fourier plane-wave series
expansion, the angular-domain sparsifying basis (e.g., DFT
basis) is imposed on HMIMO channels, demonstrating pro-
nounced power leakage. Speciﬁcally, the angular-domain
probes fail to accurately identify signiﬁcant angular power
corresponding to clusters in the EM scattering environment,
in which non-signiﬁcant angle power may be erroneously
interpreted as their signiﬁcant counterparts. On the other hand,
the inevitable mutual coupling effect cannot be sufﬁciently
resolved by a traditional sparsifying basis, causing further
sparse channel recovery difﬁculties. Furthermore, adopting a
sampling interval of less than half a wavelength not only
necessitates additional probes, leading to probe redundancy,
but also fails to improve the detection accuracy.
2) We utilize a wavenumber-domain (WD) sparsifying
basis—originally introduced in earlier literature such as [33],
[34], [35], and [36]—to unveil the sparsity inherent in HMIMO
channels and demonstrate its robustness against the key
challenges of power leakage , mutual coupling , and probe
redundancy. In our work, the WD sparsifying basis is con-
structed based on a series of Fourier harmonics (FHs), whose
number depends solely on the array aperture. This property
allows the WD basis to maintain high estimation accuracy
regardless of the number of antenna elements or their spac-
ing, in clear contrast to traditional angular-domain bases. By
leveraging the sparsity revealed in the wavenumber domain,
we formulate HMIMO channel estimation as a compressed
sensing (CS) problem, enabling a more robust and efﬁcient
estimation framework under practical hardware constraints.
3) To efﬁciently resolve the formulated CS problem, we
design an elliptical Markov random ﬁeld (EMRF) topology
to capture the clustered sparsity of the wavenumber-domain
HMIMO channel. This can be achieved by establishing
hidden binary support entries with probability distributions
for identifying wavenumber-domain non-zero entries. Neigh-
boring transition probabilities of the support entries are
modeled to reﬂect the clustered nature of the non-zero entries.
Furthermore, different from the conventional CS-based algo-
rithms using entry-by-entry searching, a novel graph-cut-based
swap expansion (GCSE) algorithm is proposed to iterate
cluster-wise, facilitating efﬁcient and robust sparse channel
estimation.
4) Simulation results are presented to validate the robustness
of our proposed GCSE algorithm in estimating HMIMO
channels. These results demonstrate its fast convergence speed,
reduced computational complexity, and applicability to chal-
lenging scenarios with low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and
limited radio-frequency (RF) chains. Additionally, we evince
that when the antenna spacing decreases far below half a wave-
length, the wavenumber-domain technique remains highly
efﬁcient in accurately reconstructing HMIMO channels. This
superiority is upheld irrespective of the sampling interval of
the basis (determined by antenna spacing) and the number of
antennas, revealing its robustness across various scenarios.

problem. In Sec. III-A, we unmask the limitations of the
traditional angular-domain sparse representation within the
HMIMO channel and propose to reformulate the sparse chan-

the problem formulation, in Sec, IV , we propose an EMRF
model to capture the clustered sparsity. Next, a novel GCSE
algorithm is proposed to achieve fast and robust sparse channel
estimation in Sec. V. Then, numerical results are presented
in Sec. VI to demonstrate the effectiveness of our pro-
posed wavenumber-domain sparse channel estimation scheme.
Finally, we conclude the paper in Sec. VII.

Nevertheless, there are three pivotal challenges associated
with deploying Ψa within the HMIMO channel model. Firstly,
the DFT-based angular-domain dictionary matrix Ψa is a
square matrix where Na = N, implying that the number of
angular probes is equivalent to the number of antenna ele-
ments. This conﬁguration introduces signiﬁcant redundancy,
rendering it inefﬁcient for sparse channel representation in the
context of the HMIMO channel model, particularly under con-
ditions of sub-half-wavelength antenna spacing—a problem
referred to as “probe redundancy.” Secondly, the sub-half-
wavelength setup leads to a mismatch between the zeros of
the Dirichlet kernel and the angular sampling probes. This
mismatch results in the “power leakage” issue, where the
signiﬁcant angular power within the angular power spectrum
(APS) corresponding to a scatterer might be misidentiﬁed
as its non-signiﬁcant counterpart. Lastly, and perhaps most
critically, is the challenge of “mutual coupling distortion.”

VII. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we proposed a novel wavenumber domain
sparse channel representation, which, compared with the
widely-used angular domain representation, enables a more
precise sparsiﬁcation for HMIMO channels by representing
the power distribution in the EM ﬁeld using the proposed FH-
based sparsifying dictionary. Aiming to excite future research
interest in sparse channel estimation for HMIMO systems, we
provide an in-depth theoretical understanding of the sparse
nature and behavior within the sub-half-wavelength HMIMO
systems. Furthermore, the proposed EMRF model combined
with the GCSE algorithm shows its capability to achieve
more robust HMIMO channel estimation with much-reduced
complexity. It is especially effective for, but not limited to,
harvesting the performance gain provided by the clustered
sparsity inherent in the HMIMO channel. Numerical sim-
ulations have validated that the proposed scheme achieves
enhanced robustness against variations in SNR, increased
antenna density, and inadequate feeds, all while requiring up
to 98% less computation time than conventional methods.
