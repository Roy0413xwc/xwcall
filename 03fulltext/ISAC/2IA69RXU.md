Abstract—A novel jitter-robust integrated sensing and commu-
nication (ISAC)-enabled beam tracking framework is proposed
for unmanned aerial vehicle (UA V)-to-UA V (U2U) communication
systems. In particular, a tethered UA V base station (UA V-B) serves
a maneuvering UA V user (UA V-U), where jitter disrupts beam
alignment and degrades communication rates. To enable beam
alignment, a UA V-U angle tracking method is ﬁrst proposed
to estimate the relative angles between UA V-B and UA V-U.
Speciﬁcally, the tethered UA V-B leverages sensing echo signals
to obtain motion information of UA V-U, and integrates them
with UA V-U’s prior motion models to enhance the accuracy of
angle prediction. Meanwhile, through the communication link,
UA V-U can receive the direction of UA V-B. Building on the
obtained angle information, a jitter compensation algorithm is
further developed to maximize the achievable rate of the U2U
communication systems. In particular, UA V-B employs adaptive
beamwidth control (ABC) to adjust its beamwidth and thus
ensures robust coverage for UA V-U. Meanwhile, UA V-U employs
beam steering reﬁnement (BSR) to optimize the direction based
on predicted angle information, enhancing the received signal-
to-noise ratio (SNR) and reducing beam misalignment caused
by jitter. Finally, our numerical results unveil that: 1) higher
angle prediction accuracy is achieved by the proposed UA V-U
angle tracking scheme compared to conventional Kalman ﬁlter-
based angle tracking methods; and 2) higher achievable rate in
the U2U communication systems is achieved by the proposed jit-
ter compensation scheme compared with traditional beamwidth
control-based methods.
Index Terms—Beam tracking, beam alignment, ISAC, jittering
effects, UA V communications.

scenarios. To suppress the pronounced free-space path loss
inherent in mmWave frequencies (30–300 GHz) [2], [3],
massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) architectures
coupled with directional beamforming techniques have been
identiﬁed as critical enablers for UA V mmWave communica-
tion systems [4], [5]. The narrow beam generated by MIMO
provides a high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and effectively
reduces severe path loss. Nevertheless, it is challenging to
align a pencil-shaped narrow beam between the UA V and the
base station (BS) because of the highly dynamic mobility of
UA Vs.
To ensure continuous beam alignment between the BS
and UA Vs in highly dynamic scenarios, beam tracking tech-
nology has attracted signiﬁcant attention from researchers
[6], [7]. Traditional beam tracking methods depend on iter-
ative feedback mechanisms through beam training protocols
[8], [9], [10]. This process involves transmitting directional
pilots and estimating channel state information (CSI) to iter-
atively ﬁnd the optimal beam alignment direction. However,
such approaches incur considerable pilots and high tempo-
ral latency, which may compromise time-sensitive alignment
opportunities.
In recent years, the emergence of integrated sensing and
communication (ISAC) technology has provided new insights
for addressing the aforementioned challenges [11], [12], [13],
[14], [15]. This technology utilizes radar functionality to
extract angular information from the radar echoes, thereby
eliminating the need for conventional pilots and improving
tracking efﬁciency [16]. Building upon this, the authors of
[17] studied the application of radar-assisted beam tracking
based on extended Kalman ﬁlter (EKF) in a line-of-sight (LoS)
channel model for multi-UA V cellular networks. Furthermore,
the work in [18] extended the simplistic LoS channel to prac-
tical multipath scenarios and proposed a ﬁne beam tracking
method based on the prediction results of the EKF. However,
the aforementioned methods are based on single-BS sensing of
UA Vs, which is limited by the accuracy of sensing. In view of
this, an ISAC-enabled multi-BS collaborative beam tracking
scheme for UA Vs has been proposed in [19]. This approach
integrates angle estimates from multiple BSs, thereby extend-
ing the sensing range of the BS and enhancing the accuracy of
sensing. Additionally, the authors of [20] addressed the inter-
region interference caused by beams from different BSs at the
edge of the coverage area using orthogonal frequency band
allocation.

The aforementioned ISAC-enabled beam tracking methods
[16], [17], [18], [19], [20] assumed that the attitude of
both communication parties remain stable during the beam
tracking process, meaning that angle of arrival (AoA) and
angle of departure (AoD) can be real-time obtained accurately.
However, for mmWave communication systems deployed on
UA Vs, the UA V’s jitter caused by air turbulence or mechan-
ical vibrations inevitably leads to ﬂuctuations in AoA/AoD,
making precise estimation challenging [21], [22], [23]. As a
result, severe beam misalignment will signiﬁcantly degrade the
performance of UA V mmWave communication systems.
Against this backdrop, existing works on UA V jitter gen-
erally focused on two aspects. On the one hand, regarding
the impact of jitter on UA V communication, the closed-form
outage probability under jitter was derived in [21] for UA V
mmWave system with a uniform linear array (ULA) and then
generalized to uniform planar array (UPA) conﬁgurations,
thereby enabling rigorous performance evaluation of UA V
mmWave communication systems. Building upon this, the
authors of [22] analyzed the secrecy performance of an air-
to-ground eavesdropping system under the inﬂuence of jitter.
Furthermore, the work of [23] demonstrated that in an air-to-
sea channel, UA V jitter has the potential to enhance received
power, and further provided a probabilistic characterization of
such performance improvement.
On the other hand, more relevant to our work is robust
beamforming for UA V mmWave links in the presence of jitter.
In [24], a robust beamforming scheme based on long short-
term memory networks was proposed to address the beam
misalignment caused by UA V jitter. To reduce the reliance
on labeled data, the authors of [25] designed a beamforming
scheme based on compressed sensing, which constructs a
partial directional beam codebook for beam searching by
integrating global positioning system (GPS) information and
inertial navigation system outputs, effectively reducing the
beam misalignment probability caused by jitter and elimi-
nating the need for labeled data. Additionally, using a wider
beamwidth during beamforming can also mitigate the effects
of jitter to some extent. For example, an analog beamformer
was designed to cover the entire potential jitter angle range
with wide beams in [26], using a method inspired by chirp
sequences. Meanwhile, the authors of [27] designed a wide
beam based on the 3σ (σ being the standard deviation of one-
dimensional AoA/AoD estimation error) criterion to reduce the
beam misalignment probability. Further, the authors of [28]
proposed an adaptive hybrid beamforming to maximize the
communication rate under asymmetric jitter for the UA V-to-
UA V (U2U) communication systems.
Based on the above discussions, existing works primarily
address jitter compensation for stationary UA Vs in hover
states, but overlook the beam tracking challenges arising from
jitter during the UA V ﬂight, which demands high real-time
performance. This issue is more critical in U2U communi-
cation systems, as jitter impacts both UA Vs. Consequently,
jitter compensation is required at both the transmitter and the
receiver to ensure accurate beam alignment. To the best of
our knowledge, there is currently no literature that studies the
ISAC-enabled beam tracking problem in U2U communication
systems under jittering effects.
To ﬁll this gap, this paper investigates a jitter-robust ISAC-
enabled beam tracking framework for U2U communication
systems, to guarantee the beam alignment between UA V
base station (UA V-B) and UA V user (UA V-U) under jitter-
ing effects. Leveraging the ISAC technique, UA V-B obtains
motion information of UA V-U from sensing echo signals to
predict the optimal beam directions for both UA Vs, and then
conveys this predicted angle information to UA V-U through
the communication link. Subsequently, to counteract the beam
misalignment caused by jitter, UA V-B performs beamwidth
adjustment along the predicted directions to enhance coverage
robustness, while UA V-U conducts beam reﬁnement to max-
imize the received SNR. For clarity, the contributions of this
paper can be summarized as follows:
• We consider a U2U communication system under jittering
effects, where UA V-B transmits the ISAC signals to UA V-
U and receives the reﬂected echoes. To ensure that the
transmit beam of UA V-B remains aligned with the receive
beam of UA V-U, we formulate an optimization problem
that aims to maximize the achievable rate between the
two UA Vs. To solve this problem, we propose a jitter-
robust ISAC-enabled beam tracking framework, which
consists of two schemes, namely the UAV-U angle track-
ing scheme and the jitter compensation scheme .
• For UA V-U angle tracking, we utilize ISAC sensing
echo signals to extract motion information, including
Doppler shifts and relative angles. By integrating these
with three predeﬁned motion models through an inter-
acting multiple-model (IMM) framework, we achieve
accurate angle predictions. Furthermore, UA V-B shares
the predicted angle information with UA V-U via the
communication link, enhancing beam tracking robustness.
• For the jitter compensation, we solve the achievable
rate maximization problem by decomposing it into two
subproblems. Speciﬁcally, UA V-B dynamically adjusts its
transmit beamwidth based on the IMM-predicted angle
through adaptive beamwidth control (ABC), ensuring
robust coverage of UA V-U despite jitter-induced devi-
ations. Meanwhile, UA V-U optimizes its receive beam
direction through beam steering reﬁnement (BSR) by
exploiting the predicted angle information, maximizing
the received SNR to compensate for the jitter-induced
beam misalignment.
• Our numerical results unveil that 1) the proposed IMM-
based angle tracking scheme achieves higher angle
prediction accuracy compared to conventional Kalman
ﬁlter-based tracking methods; and 2) the proposed jit-
ter compensation scheme, combining ABC and BSR,
signiﬁcantly outperforms traditional beamwidth control-
based jitter compensation algorithms, achieving a higher
achievable rate in the U2U communication systems.

II. S YSTEM MODEL AND GENERAL FRAMEWORK
As shown in Fig. 1, we consider a U2U communica-
tion scenario, where a tethered UA V-B communicates with
a maneuvering UA V-U. Speciﬁcally, the UA V-B transmits
the ISAC signal to UA V-U and receives the echo signals
from UA V-U. Meanwhile, the UA V-U performs multimodal
motions in the air, including constant velocity (CV) motion,
constant acceleration (CA) motion, and constant turn (CT)
motion. Under normal conditions, without jittering effects, the
beam between UA V-B and UA V-U can be precisely aligned.
However, the jittering effects will inevitably cause beam
misalignment between the two UA Vs, resulting in a decline
in the communication quality between them. Further, both
the UA Vs are equipped with the UPA with half-wavelength
spacing. To be speciﬁc, the UA V-B is equipped with a UPA
of Mx rows and 2Ny columns, which is evenly divided
into transmit and receive arrays, i.e., Nt = Mx×Ny and
Nr = Mx×Ny, respectively. The UA V-U is also equipped
with a UPA of Nu =Mx×Ny antennas on the bottom of its
body. Furthermore, the locations of UA V-B and UA V-U at the
nth epoch can be represented as pB,n = (0, 0, 0) and pU,n =
(xU,n,yU,n, zU,n), respectively. Let us denote the azimuth
angle, elevation angle, velocity and distance of UA V-U
relative to UA V-B as θ(t), ϕ(t), v(t) and d(t), respectively.
Note that these parameters are functions of time t∈ [0,T ],
with T being the maximum time of interest. For notational
convenience, we discretize T into N small time slots with
a length of ∆T , where each ∆T is an epoch. Following
the standard assumption in the literature [16], [17], [18], we
assume that the motion parameters remain constant within
each ∆T . Therefore, the motion parameters at the nth epoch
can be denoted as θn, ϕn, vn, and dn.

D. General Framework
To address the above problem, we propose a jitter-robust
ISAC-enabled beam tracking framework for U2U communica-
tion. As shown in Fig. 2, it is assumed that UA V-B and UA V-U
have initially established a connection through beam training.
Due to the movement of UA V-U and jittering effects, UA V-B
needs to perform beam tracking for UA V-U periodically to
maximize the achievable rate during data transmission. The
speciﬁc content of the framework is as follows:
1) UAV-U Angle Tracking: As shown in Fig. 2(a), to ensure
that the transmit beam of UA V-B is always directed towards
UA V-U, UA V-U needs to be tracked. Speciﬁcally, UA V-B
ﬁrst utilizes the echo signals from UA V-U in the previous
period, combining with IMM to achieve prediction of UA V-B’s
transmitting beam direction (i.e. ˆθn|n−1, ˆϕn|n−1) at the current
epoch, as well as prediction of UA V-U’s receiving beam direc-
tion (i.e. ˆθn+1|n−1, ˆϕn+1|n−1) at the next epoch, preparing for
the subsequent ABC and BSR algorithms.
2) Adaptive Beamwidth Control of UAV-B: As shown in
Fig. 2(b), jittering will lead to the transmit beam deviat-
ing from the desired direction. Therefore, UA V-B needs to
adaptively adjust the transmit beamwidth at the transmitting
beam direction to achieve robust coverage for UA V-U, and the
predicted receiving angles will be sent to the UA V-U.
3) Beam Steering Reﬁnement of UAV-U: At the (n− 1)th
epoch, the UA V-U receives a data sequence containing the
predicted receiving beam direction (i.e. ˆθn|n−2, ˆϕn|n−2),
which will be exploited for UA V-U’s receiving beamforming
at the nth epoch. However, the jittering effects will cause
the receive beam of UA V-U to deviate from the expected
direction. Nevertheless, ˆθn|n−2 and ˆϕn|n−2 can be used as
prior information of jitter compensation. As shown in Fig. 2(c),
UA V-U can search within an interval centered around ˆθn|n−2
and ˆϕn|n−2 to ﬁnd the direction that maximizes the received
SNR, thereby constructing the receive beam.

where UA V-U utilizes the prediction results from the previous
epoch or expands the search range. Although this may slightly
increase the computational load, it ensures that the beam

Analysis
1) Convergence Analysis: In the case of limited iterations
and particle numbers, the PSO algorithm progressively nar-
rows the search space by updating the particles’ positions
and velocities, gradually approaching the optimal solution.
This gives the PSO algorithm inherent convergence properties.
Furthermore, the proposed CDPSO algorithm incorporates a
coordinate descent strategy, optimizing one dimension at a
time, which stabilizes the convergence process. As a result,
the algorithm converges to a local optimum, which is typically
a near-optimal solution. Thus, the CDPSO algorithm ensures
stable convergence in practical applications.

VI. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, a novel jitter-robust ISAC-enabled beam
tracking framework for U2U communication systems was
proposed, where UA V-B transmits ISAC signals to UA V-U
and receives the echoes reﬂected from UA V-U. To track the
relative angles of UA V-U, an IMM-based tracking scheme
was introduced. Speciﬁcally, UA V-B obtains the motion infor-
mation of UA V-U through sensing echo signals, and then
improves the angle prediction accuracy by fusing three pre-
deﬁned motion models. Based on the angles predicted by
the IMM, a jitter compensation algorithm was proposed to
maximize the achievable rate. Speciﬁcally, an ABC algorithm
that dynamically adjusts the beamwidth according to jitter
levels was proposed for UA V-B, while a BSR algorithm that
searches for the direction maximizing the received SNR was
proposed for UA V-U. Finally, simulation results demonstrated
that the proposed framework achieves accurate tracking of
UA V-U and superior jitter compensation performance. In our
future work, the proposed framework will be extended to
multi-user scenarios. In such cases, inter-user interference can
be effectively mitigated by employing spatial division multiple
access (SDMA).
